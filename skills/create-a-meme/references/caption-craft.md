# Caption craft — how to make a meme land

The image is the delivery; the caption is the joke. Most bad memes fail on the
words, not the render.

## The one rule: find the turn
Every good meme has an *unexpected beat* — the gap between setup and payoff.
State it in one sentence before you write anything. No turn, no meme.

## Structure
- **Two-panel (top/bottom):** top = setup/expectation, bottom = the twist.
- **Reaction/single:** the caption is the thought the face is having.
- **Keep each line to ~2–6 words.** If it needs a comma splice, cut it.

## Do
- **One idea per meme.** Two jokes cancel out.
- **Be specific.** "deploying on a Friday" beats "doing risky things."
- **Punch down the expected, up the absurd** — the funniest line is the one the
  reader didn't see coming but instantly recognizes.
- **Read it aloud.** If you don't smirk, rewrite the words, not the image.

## Don't
- Don't explain the joke in the caption. Trust the reader.
- Don't write paragraphs. A meme is a glance, not a read.
- Don't chase "pretty." A crisp render of a flat joke is still a flat joke.
- Don't over-apologize or hedge. Offer another take instead.

## Iterating
Regenerate for *wording* first, *template* second, *image* last. If two attempts
miss, the idea is the problem — go back to the turn.
